Girdwood's second gas line

Enstar wants to build a backup natural gas line into Girdwood through the forest near Virgin Creek. The Heritage Land Bank Advisory Commission holds a public hearing on part of the route on Thursday, November 19, 2026. Here is what is known, with sources.

Compiled November 15, 2026 by a Girdwood resident from public records. Not affiliated with Enstar, the Municipality or the State.

Summary

Almost all of Girdwood's gas comes through one 6-inch plastic main that runs from Enstar's regulator station on Ruane Road up the Alyeska Highway.3 If that main is cut, everyone downstream of the break loses gas. Enstar wants to add a second, 4-inch line from the end of Ruane Road, under Glacier Creek, to Virgin Creek Road, so gas can reach the valley from both ends (a "two-way feed").5,9▸49:04

Enstar first planned to build the whole line inside a state section-line easement that runs east–west across Heritage Land Bank land. The plan was 4,850 feet of 4-inch pipe: 1,650 feet drilled underground (mostly a 1,500-foot bore under Glacier Creek and its wetlands), and 3,350 feet in a trench, in a 20-foot-wide strip cleared west from the end of Virgin Creek Road.25p.5 The cleared strip would cross the Lower Virgin Creek and Joe Danich trails through old-growth forest.4,29p.4 Residents and the Girdwood Board of Supervisors (GBOS) objected in 2023. The state Department of Natural Resources (DNR) issued a letter of non-objection in August 2024, and neighbors appealed.29p.4,8

The new item is HLB Resolution 2026-11. It would grant Enstar a permanent, non-exclusive gas easement plus a temporary construction easement on part of Heritage Land Bank Parcel 6-062 (Tract 20-A). The easement is 20 feet wide and about 1,000 feet long, between the section-line easement and Virgin Creek Road. HLB describes it as an alternative to placing the project in the state-managed section-line easement.1 Without the staff report, I can't tell exactly which stretch it replaces. The 2023 plan had to leave the section line at the east end to reach the tie-in on Virgin Creek Road (where the green line turns south on the map), and this easement is probably related to that connection. That is my inference.

Questions and answers

Each answer is labeled by how solid it is: Documented means a public record says so. Estimate is my own rough arithmetic from those records. Not public means I couldn't find the answer, and it is a good question to ask on Nov 19.

What is the line for? Is it only redundancy?

Documented Enstar's stated purpose is reliability. Adding a second feed means that a break on the existing main doesn't cut off everyone downstream. Enstar calls Girdwood "a prime example" of an area that needs this. In 2022 it built over 40,000 feet of main elsewhere to give other single-feed areas a second feed.3

Growth is part of the reason too. In written answers to GBOS in July 2023, Enstar said that "as the Girdwood community continues to expand and demand for natural gas grows," more load falls on the single feed.27 Enstar's letter says the Nordic Spa at Hotel Alyeska added "significant additional load," and customers on the main grew 7% from 2016 to 2023.3 The GBOS minutes record the purpose as ensuring reliability and improving "low flow of gas to some parts of the valley."28 Enstar also calls two-way feeds an industry best practice for keeping pressure high enough at the end of the line in winter.29p.17

Enstar has also said the line would help keep pressure up, which matters most in cold weather when demand peaks.16 At the July 2023 meeting, Enstar's engineer said growth and added load on the system were part of why the project had "come to a head." When explaining why a more northern route would work less well, he noted it wouldn't add capacity for "future expansion."9▸1:24:17▸1:45:54 A resident who called DNR reported being told the reasons were low gas pressure in that area and future development.9▸1:33:04 So redundancy is the main stated goal, but capacity for growth and pressure at the far ends of the system are also part of it.

Enstar's answer at GBOS (48:02) · engineer on history and load (1:23:02) · engineer on route and expansion (1:45:14)

What it doesn't protect against: The new line starts at the same Ruane Road regulator station as the existing main.9▸48:04 It protects against a break on the distribution main inside the valley. It wouldn't help if the transmission line along Turnagain Arm failed between Anchorage and Girdwood, which would cut off both Girdwood and Whittier.16
How likely is the main line to fail, and how long would a fix take?

How a line "goes out." Documented Nationally, excavation damage is a leading cause of life-threatening pipeline incidents.20 Enstar says excavators hit its pipes "over 100 times" across its system most years, and that 45% of hits from 2016 to 2022 happened between October and March. It also lists earthquakes, leaks and washouts.3 Girdwood's own record shows two kinds: a contractor strike in 2016 and a flood washout in 2021.3 Whittier's 2026 outage came from a submerged river crossing that was exposed and moving in the current.16

Probability. Estimate Enstar hasn't published a failure probability for the Girdwood main. Here are two rough ways to estimate it:

Girdwood history: about 1 serious hit on the 6" main in 30 years (1996–2026) → about 1 in 30 per year → P(at least one such hit in 10 years) ≈ 1 − e^(−10/30) ≈ 28% Enstar system-wide: about 100+ hits/yr on 3,200+ miles of main → about 0.03 hits per mile per year (an upper bound: many hits are on service lines) × a few miles of main along Alyeska Hwy (my rough guess) × 10 years → on the order of 1 hit per decade

Both methods suggest that some damage to the main within a decade is plausible, maybe 1 in 4 or higher. But most hits don't cause a valley-wide outage: in 2016 service held. An outage needs a break severe enough that Enstar has to shut the line off, especially in winter. My guess for that is well under the 28% figure, perhaps 5–15% over ten years. Treat this as an order of magnitude, not a forecast.

Time to fix. Documented Enstar estimates 4–7 days to restore service after a full outage, and months to reconnect some seasonal properties. The slow part is relighting: a technician has to turn each meter back on and relight the appliances.3 Enstar's engineer warned that in a cold snap, houses could freeze before crews reach them.9▸1:23:58 For comparison, Whittier was without gas from Aug 16 to Aug 23, 2026, for 39 meters, and only because Enstar trucked in LNG from Alberta.16,17,18 Girdwood has about 40 times as many meters.

How does Girdwood compare with other towns? Do most have two feeds?

Not public I found no statistic on what fraction of towns, or of Enstar's service area, have redundant feeds. Enstar calls a secondary feed "a best practice in the gas pipeline" industry and says it builds them for areas that only have one line.3

Documented The closest example is Whittier, at the end of the same Turnagain Arm feed. After the August 2026 river-crossing break, the whole town was without gas for about a week. Enstar restored service only by trucking LNG and regasification equipment from Edmonton, and it says repair costs will be spread across all ratepayers.16 Locally, in 2026 Enstar also had to reroute a gas line at Rabbit Creek in Anchorage using horizontal directional drilling.19

Who pays, and how much would Girdwood rates go up?

Documented Enstar said it is "capitalizing the project."9▸1:22:44 For a regulated utility, that means the cost goes into its rate base and is recovered over many years through rates the Regulatory Commission of Alaska (RCA) approves for its whole system. Enstar's rate cases cover all of its customers (over 150,000).21 I found no sign of a Girdwood-only surcharge. Similarly, Enstar says the Whittier repair costs will be spread across all ratepayers.16

Not public Enstar hasn't disclosed a cost estimate. DNR said it doesn't require one and that cost and how Enstar pays are outside its decision.5,29p.5 A resident at the 2023 meeting said it would cost "millions."9▸1:59:45

Estimate For a sense of scale, assume it costs $2–5 million. A utility typically collects roughly 10–15% of a capital project's cost per year in rates (return, depreciation, taxes):

$2M–$5M × 10–15% per year ≈ $200k–$750k per year ÷ ~150,000 customers ≈ $1.30–$5 per customer per year ≈ $0.10–$0.40 per month, the same in Girdwood as in Anchorage

So the bill impact is likely small for everyone. If Girdwood customers paid the whole cost alone, it would be about 100 times more per customer, but nothing suggests that's how it would be billed.

Could the corridor be an emergency road if the Alyeska Highway bridge is out?

Documented Not as proposed. The line would go under Glacier Creek by horizontal directional drilling, so no bridge would be built.9▸50:03 The cleared strip would be about 15–20 feet wide, reseeded, and left for alders to grow back.9▸1:03:01▸1:04:17 When asked in 2023 about pairing the work with a second access road, Enstar said that plan didn't fit its timeline or its system.9▸54:25▸55:11

That said, the section-line easement itself is 100 feet wide, and its primary legal purpose is public highway access. DNR noted that it "can be developed" as a road under state law.29p.7 So the gas line doesn't create a road, but it wouldn't rule one out in the same easement. A road would need its own bridge over Glacier Creek, design, funding and permits.

Will the public be able to use the corridor afterward? For what?

Documented Enstar said it can't speak to future uses. It would restore the land as its permits require, mulching cleared brush on site and reseeding.9▸1:01:23 Its engineer said he didn't know whether people would start using it as a trail.9▸1:04:25 The section-line easement itself is a public access easement, and the state says such easements are "reserved for public use" and can't be obstructed.22 DNR said construction would temporarily block access to the Lower Virgin Creek, Joe Danich and Lower Iditarod trails, but not permanently. It also said none of these trails are legally protected rights-of-way.29p.4p.11 During construction, ADF&G asked that anglers' access to Glacier and California creeks not be restricted, since coho run there from late July to early September.29p.3

Not public I couldn't find which activities would be allowed on the new HLB easement. That depends on HLB's easement terms and the Municipality's rules for the land. GBOS supervisor Brian Burnett suggested the corridor could support more trails.11 Neighbors and the Trails Committee chair worried it would damage the primitive character of the Lower Virgin Creek and Joe Danich trails.4,9▸1:05:47▸1:38:25 A former Trails Committee chair appears to say (the transcript is garbled here) that the trail from Virgin Creek to Ruane was set aside for non-motorized use.9▸1:30:37

How often would Enstar run snowmachines there? Could anyone else?

Documented At the meeting, Enstar said it does leak surveys about every four years. Its engineer expected those to be done on foot, possibly by snowmachine in winter once the ground firms up, but "likely on foot" because the line is short.9▸1:02:23▸1:03:30 The meeting minutes instead say "annually, probably on foot."28 Enstar's written answer only says surveys follow PHMSA's schedule.27 Either way, that means a visit once every one to four years, probably on foot. Construction is another matter: wetland sections would use construction mats under the equipment.9▸57:30

Documented On other people's snowmachines: a commenter asked DNR to limit future mechanical access. DNR declined and said "the section line easement does not preclude motorized use as a public highway."29p.15 Not public Whether Municipal or HLB rules restrict motorized use on the new HLB easement or the surrounding open space is a separate question I couldn't answer. Ask HLB.

What has Enstar done about bedrock, boulders and old-growth trees that could cause delays?

Documented Enstar said it would pick the tie-in point after boring and soil analysis.3 Its permits only let it build in the alignment it applied for, so any change, including for constructability, means reapplying.9▸1:12:59 A neighbor told GBOS the route on the bench runs through a boulder field that probably extends well below the surface.9▸1:08:48 In 2023, the only on-the-ground work residents had seen was by surveyors.9▸1:12:37

Commenters raised shallow bedrock and difficult terrain with DNR. DNR said it doesn't require subsurface investigations with an application, and that Enstar's design and terminus information was enough for its limited decision.29p.6 Enstar's design calls for 4 feet of cover over trenched pipe. The Glacier Creek bore would be at least 10 feet below the streambed, with the drill rig set 200 feet back from the high-water line.25p.2 Since then, Enstar has concluded it doesn't need to cross Virgin Creek, which removes one of the two bores.29p.1

Not public I found no geotechnical report or contingency plan for this project. For comparison, Enstar's 2026 Rabbit Creek reroute in Anchorage was delayed by drill rig delivery and a contractor change. Its fallback was to pull the pipe and redesign over the winter if the drilling couldn't be done.19

Not public No tree count has been published, for old-growth trees or any others.

Documented The 2023 plan cleared 3,350 feet × 20 feet, starting at the end of Virgin Creek Road and heading west. The 1,650 feet that would be drilled (Glacier Creek and its wetlands) weren't to be cleared, apart from the drill pits.25p.5

Estimate The areas involved:

2023 cleared strip: 20 ft × 3,350 ft = 67,000 sq ft ≈ 1.5 acres 2023 total easement asked: 20 ft × 4,850 ft = 97,000 sq ft ≈ 2.2 acres (DNR: 2.23) HLB Parcel 6-062 easement: 20 ft × 1,000 ft = 20,000 sq ft ≈ 0.46 acre
